# Project Schedule and Sprint Plan: EOP

Ten weeks, five two-week sprints after a short initiation and design phase. Dates are shown as week numbers, so the plan holds for any project start date.

| Phase | Weeks | Key tasks | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initiation | 1 | Charter, business case, stakeholder register | Morenikeji | Approved charter |
| Planning | 1-2 | Scope, WBS, backlog, risks, comms, test plan | Morenikeji, Francisca | Baselined plan |
| Design | 2-3 | User flows, Google Stitch prototype, review | Francisca, team | Approved prototype |
| Sprint 1 | 3-4 | Authentication and roles | Olabisi, team | US-01 done |
| Sprint 2 | 5-6 | Checklists, task assignment, completion | Olabisi, team | US-02, US-03 done |
| Sprint 3 | 6-7 | Document upload and review | Olabisi, team | US-04 done |
| Sprint 4 | 7-8 | Dashboard and notifications | Olabisi, team | US-05, US-06 done |
| Sprint 5 | 8-9 | Hardening, content pages, reports, regression | Precious, team | Release candidate |
| UAT | 9 | Acceptance testing with product owner | Precious, Francisca | UAT sign-off |
| Release | 10 | Deploy, user guide, handover | Olabisi, Morenikeji | Live portal |
| Closure | 10 | Closure report, lessons learned | Morenikeji | Signed closure |

## Milestones

| ID | Milestone | Week | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Charter approved | 1 | Sponsor sign-off |
| M2 | Plan and backlog baselined | 2 | Product owner agreement |
| M3 | Prototype approved | 3 | Sponsor review |
| M4 | Core features complete | 7 | Sprint review |
| M5 | Release candidate | 9 | Test exit criteria met |
| M6 | UAT sign-off | 9 | Sponsor and product owner |
| M7 | Go-live and closure | 10 | Final sign-off |

## Sprint ceremonies

- Sprint planning: first day of each sprint
- Daily stand-up: 15 minutes, on the team chat or a short call
- Sprint review: last day of each sprint, with sponsor and product owner invited
- Retrospective: after each review

## Critical path

Prototype approval, then authentication, then task assignment, then document handling, then UAT. A slip in authentication delays every later sprint, so it is built first.
